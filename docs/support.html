MacRemote Support

Your iPhone as a trackpad and keyboard for your Mac.

Setting up

  1. On the Mac, download MacRemote from the releases page and drag it into Applications.
  2. Open it. It appears in the menu bar — there is no window and no Dock icon.
  3. Click the menu bar icon and grant Accessibility when asked. macOS requires this before any app may move the pointer or type; the panel has a button that opens the right settings page.
  4. On the iPhone, open MacRemote, tap your Mac, and enter the six-digit code the Mac shows.

The iPhone does not see my Mac

  • Both devices have to be on the same Wi-Fi network. A phone on mobile data, or on a guest network, will not find anything.
  • The Mac app has to be running — look for its icon in the menu bar.
  • The first time, iOS asks for permission to find devices on the local network. If you declined, turn it back on in Settings → MacRemote → Local Network.
  • Some routers block devices from seeing each other. If yours has a setting called client isolation, AP isolation or guest mode, turn it off for your network.

The pointer does not move

The Accessibility permission is missing or was reset. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility and make sure MacRemote is on. If it is already on, switch it off and on again — macOS ties the permission to the exact copy of the app, and it can go stale after an update.

It says the Mac no longer recognises this iPhone

The pairing was removed on the Mac. Open the menu bar panel, allow new devices, and pair again with a fresh code.

The Mac refuses to pair

By design, the Mac stops accepting new devices once the first one has paired. Open the menu bar panel and turn accepting new devices back on.

Requirements

  • iPhone with iOS 18 or later
  • Mac with macOS 14 or later
  • Both on the same Wi-Fi network
